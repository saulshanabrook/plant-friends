# Plant Friends: what a match score can honestly say

Research date: 2026-10-17 America/Los_Angeles. This combines independent source research, an actual Claude Fable consultation, and a small reproducible re-scoring experiment. It does **not** fit a calibrator, establish an unknown-species threshold, or change deployed identification scores. Model/library details, immutable source links, runnable Python examples and existing app precedents are in [the BioCLIP source review](bioclip2-similarity-sources.md).

## Recommendation

Show **“Possible catalog matches”** as an ordered list with equal visual treatment. For a later presentation change, omit numbers from the default list; expose **“Cosine similarity”** in an optional details disclosure, with “Ranks names in this catalog; not a probability.” Keep the photo, names and reference records easy to inspect. First position already communicates order; a green badge, checkmark, percentage bar or “high confidence” label would imply an acceptance decision we have not validated. These are product recommendations, not a claim that a particular wording has passed usability testing.

Do not add a universal cutoff, transform cosine into a percent, or adopt another demo's color thresholds. A species absent from the catalog currently still gets five suggestions. Say **“No supported match”** only after a separately evaluated rejection rule exists; do not assert “not a plant” from a low score alone. Retain one concise statement separating possible identity from safety to eat or use medicinally. PFAF ratings are properties of the selected record, not additional evidence that the photo depicts that record.

This change set leaves current ranking and the existing three-decimal similarity display intact while documenting the decision. No invented confidence/rejection behavior is being shipped.

## Current math, official Python and percentages

The app computes `c_i = normalize(image) · v_i`. Each `v_i` is a normalized mean of 80 normalized scientific-name prompt vectors. It searches all **8,796 PFAF records**, then displays five. Cosine is in `[-1,1]`; neither `0.74 → 74%` nor `(c+1)/2` is a correctness calculation. Our labels include aliases, hybrids and cultivars, so catalog rows are not an exhaustive list of mutually exclusive biological species. See [browser ranking](../src/inference/scoring.ts) and [asset construction](../tools/plant_assets/model.py).

Official `pybioclip` 2.1.6 instead applies candidate softmax to scaled cosines. For our pinned checkpoint the independently read scale is approximately 100:

```text
z_i = 100 c_i
q_i = exp(z_i − max(z)) / sum_over_all_candidates exp(z_j − max(z))
```

This preserves ranking. It allocates all mass to the supplied candidates and has no unknown bucket. Its numerical value depends on the candidate list, prompt construction and temperature. `TreeOfLifeClassifier` can sum child probabilities for higher ranks; its `min_prob` option is an aggregation optimization, not a validated rejection threshold. The official demo and Image Search Lite offer normalized score bars; Lite's 50%/10% color changes are presentation rules. The [released implementation](https://github.com/Imageomics/pybioclip/blob/256e467401a57c5e2cd8acb1467fbd834d757e9d/src/bioclip/predict.py#L222-L243) and [actual Lite display code](https://huggingface.co/spaces/imageomics/bioclip-image-search-lite/blob/9a45f1a4e348b2f997b3f3a39711ac645ee372a6/app.py#L651-L695) provide precedent for **relative scores**, not evidence of PFAF correctness calibration.

Adding a constant to every cosine leaves softmax unchanged. Removing rival labels increases the remaining shares; duplicating one label changes their allocation. A single-candidate softmax is always 1. A top-two margin `c_1−c_2` describes separation, not absolute fit; it can be large when every candidate is wrong and small between synonyms. Entropy `−Σq_i log(q_i)` describes concentration under that same candidate list/temperature. Agreement across crops/prompts can be consistently wrong. None supplies a new source of identity truth merely by being computed.

A fitted temperature would use `q_i(T)=softmax(100c_i/T)`, with positive `T` selected on held-out labeled calibration examples, usually by negative log likelihood. The checkpoint's contrastive-training scale is **not** that deployment calibration. Temperature changes uncertainty estimates, not argmax accuracy. [Guo et al., 2017](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v70/guo17a.html) establish the standard post-hoc method; [Tu et al., ICML 2024](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v235/tu24a.html) specifically find that VLMs are not inherently calibrated and study temperature scaling across domain, label and hierarchy shifts. Tu et al. found aggregate calibration error plateauing after roughly 40–50 calibration images in several tested tasks (section5.3). That encourages a small temperature-fit pilot, but is a different objective from proving low accepted-error rates across rare taxa and unknown species. Their experiments support trying this baseline; they do not establish a BioCLIP 2/PFAF guarantee or make absent species expressible by a closed catalog softmax.

## What our existing evidence says

The prior [desktop benchmark](desktop-benchmark.json) contains 24 positive photos across 12 names: top-one 14/24, top-five 21/24. Four additional absent-species/non-plant photos all receive plant suggestions. Unknown training overlap, small size and nonrepresentative sampling make this descriptive evidence, not an accuracy promise or calibration set.

A correct top-one has cosine **0.7021**; an incorrect apple identification has **0.7432**. An absent orchid, *Phalaenopsis amabilis*, gets **0.7072** for *Cyanella orchidiformis*. Thus a proposed `0.70` cutoff already accepts both errors and rejects no high-scoring mistake. Raising it can discard correct examples while retaining wrong ones. These counterexamples invalidate that particular shortcut; they do not prove every learned use of cosine must fail.

I re-scored ten **existing saved FP32 embeddings**, checking their SHA-256 receipts and the full text-vector matrix. No image encoder, model download or additional photo collection was needed. This is analytic float64 scoring of stored float32 values, not a new browser-accuracy run. [Script](similarity-score-experiment.py), [complete results and input hashes](similarity-score-experiment.json):

| Existing fixture | Top suggestion | Cosine | Full 8,796-label softmax, scale 100 | Softmax incorrectly restricted to top five |
| --- | --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| `50800`, labeled *Plantago major* | *Plantago rugelii* — incorrect top-one | 0.7539 | 0.9370 | 0.9514 |
| `22158`, labeled *Bellis perennis* | *Bellis perennis* | 0.7165 | 0.9922 | 0.9966 |
| `16418`, labeled *Trifolium repens* | *Trifolium obtusiflorum* — incorrect top-one | 0.7106 | 0.3299 | 0.4253 |

The wrong *Plantago* suggestion demonstrates that a high **93.7% relative score can still be wrong**. One wrong outcome alone does not prove miscalibration—a calibrated 93.7% group still includes errors—but no held-out study here measured its correctness probability. The synthetic fixture illustrates denominator effects; it is not a labeled OOD benchmark. `landscape` and its lossless WebP share one embedding and must not be counted as independent observations. Reproduce from prepared local assets with `.venv/bin/python reports/similarity-score-experiment.py`.

## Options for uncertainty and unknown inputs

| Approach | What is established elsewhere | Fit and unresolved work here |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Maximum cosine, margin, entropy, maximum full-catalog softmax | Simple deterministic scores; useful baselines to compare empirically. | Cheap offline; overlap above already rules out interpreting a chosen raw number as correctness. Fit/test rejection separately. |
| MCM | [Ming et al., NeurIPS 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.13445) study maximum concept matching for VLM OOD detection. The [authors' implementation](https://github.com/deeplearning-wisc/MCM/blob/main/utils/detection_util.py#L225-L248) softmaxes **cosines divided by τ**, then takes maximum (code negates it to orient larger values toward OOD); [default τ is 1](https://github.com/deeplearning-wisc/MCM/blob/main/eval_ood_detection.py#L28-L34). | This is not automatically BioCLIP's `softmax(100c)`. Compare both explicitly on PFAF and hard absent relatives; its benchmark success does not supply our threshold. No extra image encoder is needed. |
| Negative labels | [Jiang et al., NegLabel, ICLR 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.20078) use a broad negative vocabulary and a designed OOD score. Their [source](https://github.com/tmlr-group/NegLabel/blob/main/mmcls/models/classifiers/multi_modal.py#L118-L175) constructs/selects negative text embeddings and computes both positive/negative similarities. | A promising next experiment: precompute absent plant species plus animal/object/background distractor vectors. Keep these separate from selectable PFAF records. A few generic words like “unknown plant” are not a faithful NegLabel implementation or a comprehensive unknown class. Scientific-name ambiguity and missing negative concepts remain risks. |
| Genus/family fallback | Official BioCLIP tools aggregate taxonomic children; broader groups can represent ambiguity without choosing a species. | Build an explicit canonical taxonomy/alias map first, validate at each rank, and report a broader rank only if its own acceptance rule passes. A string prefix or PFAF family field is not a calibrated hierarchy. Changing candidate counts affects sums. |
| Selective prediction | [Geifman and El-Yaniv, 2017](https://papers.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2017/hash/4a8423d5e91fda00bb7e46540e2b0cf1-Abstract.html) study trading accepted coverage for error among accepted predictions. | Best match for “highlight supported suggestions”: choose an acceptable error objective, then validate a fixed scoring/acceptance rule and report risk–coverage. Separately measure acceptance of absent species. A low in-catalog error alone does not establish unknown rejection. |
| Conformal sets | [Angelopoulos and Bates](https://arxiv.org/html/2107.07511v6) describe held-out calibration giving marginal coverage under exchangeable calibration/test examples. | Could produce variable-length sets of plausible names; not a per-photo posterior or guaranteed correct singleton. A top-five cap can destroy coverage. An absent true label cannot be included in a set drawn only from the catalog. Distribution changes need additional methods/assumptions; an empty set is not intrinsically proof of a non-plant. |

These are alternative experiments, not a list of dependencies to add to the PWA. MCM and small acceptance/calibration functions could reuse existing image embeddings. Negative vectors or a broader taxonomy require new versioned static assets, not a Python serving process. New model, prompt, preprocessing, candidate or canonicalization versions require fresh validation.

## Practical validation plan before adding confidence or highlights

This is a proposed experiment, not completed evidence or a requirement to finish the current UI work.

1. **Define the target.** Distinguish correct PFAF record retrieval, accepted biological identity at species/genus rank, and rejection of inputs whose true species has no suitable catalog record. Decide how aliases, cultivars and hybrids count before scoring. Use expert/independently verified labels; “Research Grade” is useful provenance, not infallibility. Multiple plants and unidentifiable views should have explicit labels rather than forced species truth.
2. **Build independent groups.** Start with hundreds to low thousands of diverse observations across many represented taxa and hard absent near relatives, plus non-plants, poor light, close/partial views, cultivated plants and multiple subjects. This is a useful pilot size, not sufficient evidence for all 8,796 taxa. Keep all photos of an observation/individual and near duplicates in one partition; group photographer/site/time when practical. Record species/source/license and potential pretraining overlap. Test newly collected or documented post-training material separately where possible; lack of overlap knowledge must remain disclosed.
3. **Separate development, calibration and final evaluation.** Use development data to select features/method/negative labels, a disjoint calibration split for temperature and acceptance thresholds, and a locked independent test split for reported performance. Do not repeatedly tune against the final test set. Hold out whole absent taxa/sites as shift stress tests; avoid calling a taxon with no calibration support “validated” because aggregate metrics are good. Compare macro taxon performance and observation-weighted performance.
4. **Keep baselines small.** Compare current ranking, cosine/margin, full-catalog scale-100 maximum, MCM at a separately declared τ, and one precomputed-negative experiment. Add a low-capacity correctness/acceptance model only if useful; freeze features and train/calibrate without test leakage. Temperature alone is a strong first calibration baseline for in-catalog labels. It cannot represent an excluded true species.
5. **Evaluate the actual promise.** Report top-one/top-five retrieval, per-rank identity accuracy, reliability plots with sample counts/intervals, NLL/Brier and ECE (ECE alone hides bin/subgroup failures). For abstention, show accepted error versus coverage; for unknowns, AUROC/AUPR **and** false acceptance at the chosen operating point, with absent relatives separate from easy birds/objects. Report slices by taxon, image quality and domain. Deployment unknown prevalence changes precision; benchmark class balancing must be stated.
6. **Require evidence for an operating point.** Pick the tolerated accepted-error/unknown-acceptance objectives before threshold selection and test them with uncertainty bounds. As an illustration only: with a fixed rule and independent representative accepted test draws from the declared population, zero errors among 299 yields a one-sided 95% binomial upper error bound below 1%, since `1−0.05^(1/299) ≈ 0.00997`. It establishes neither zero risk nor the same bound for every species, every future phone photo or a repeatedly tuned rule. Clustered observations or shift weaken that interpretation. More data/stratified evaluation may be necessary; there is no universal “minimum 100 photos” entitlement to show confidence.
7. **Ship a small, versioned offline decision.** Store only validated coefficients/temperature/thresholds, candidate/taxonomy identities and evaluation metadata with the static pack. Keep suggestions accessible if the acceptance rule abstains; a failed rule should say it could not support a match, not claim the species is absent. Revalidate after any substantive model/catalog/prompt change. Never use PFAF edibility ratings to turn identification confidence into a consumption recommendation.

## Independent Fable consultation

The consultation was launched as an actual `claude -p --model fable --effort max` subprocess, with no tools/MCP/session persistence. It received the app equations, restricted-catalog caveats, observed counterexamples and primary-source list. It was explicitly told it could not browse. Its response is advisory critique, not an independently retrieved source or experimental evidence. It completed successfully and reported **`claude-fable-5-1`**. [Consultation record](claude-fable-similarity.md) records its recommendations and our corrections.

Fable independently favored equal-weight ranked suggestions, no default numeric score, and no arbitrary abstention threshold. It proposed resolving synonyms, adding absent-species scientific-name competitors, and evaluating difficult near relatives before calibrated highlighting. These align with the source-backed direction above; its prediction that expanded negative names will outperform other methods remains an untested hypothesis.

I did **not** adopt its blanket statements that hybrids/cultivars cannot be identified from photos, that genus summation automatically fixes alias duplication, that all wild phone inputs necessarily violate exchangeability, or that contrastive training optimizes only ordering. Those claims require qualification: identify botanical units explicitly, avoid double-counting aliases, state distribution assumptions, and distinguish ranked inference from a loss that depends on logit values. Its remembered account of MCM also omitted the exact τ distinction and overstated a far-OOD-only characterization; our inspected source above supplies the equation, and hard relatives remain necessary local tests. No automatic taxonomy collapse, quantization or model change follows from this consultation.
